<h1 style="text-align: center;" markdown="1"> Abstract</h1>
<br>
We compare how well agents aggregate information in two repeated
social learning environments. In the first setting agents have access
to a public data set. In the second they have access to the same data,
and also to the past actions of others. Despite the fact that actions
contain no additional payoff relevant information, and despite
potential herd behavior, free riding and information overload issues,
observing and imitating the actions of others leads agents to take the
optimal action more often in the second setting. We also investigate
the effect of group size, as well as a setting in which agents observe
private data and others' actions.
